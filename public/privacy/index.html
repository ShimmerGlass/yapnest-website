Privacy policy

Last updated: 2026-10-27

Who is responsible: Mathilde Gilles, a private individual in France (see the legal notice). Contact: support@yapnest.fr.

Yapnest is a chat app. It is a client for XMPP, an open messaging standard. Yapnest has no analytics, no tracking and no ads. Your messages do not go through us.

The short version

  • Your account, contacts and messages are on the XMPP server you sign in to, not on a server of ours.
  • The app keeps a copy on your device.
  • Messages in one-to-one chats are end-to-end encrypted with OMEMO, unless you turn it off.
  • Push notifications go through a small relay we run. It handles your device token and your account address. It throws away anything a server adds about a message, such as the sender and the text.
  • GIF search and link previews contact other services. You can turn both off.

Your XMPP server

When you sign in, the app connects to the server of your account. That server is run by someone else. It stores your account, your contact list, your messages (possibly as an archive) and the files you upload. It also sees who you talk to and when.

The server’s operator is responsible for that data. Read their privacy policy. Yapnest cannot see or change what your server does.

On your device

  • The app keeps a database for each account: messages, contacts, settings and OMEMO sessions.
  • Your password and sign-in details are kept in the Keychain.
  • Files you send or receive are kept on the device.
  • The app’s notification and share extensions use the same storage, so they can show and send messages.
  • The key that identifies this device is in the Keychain, on this device only.
  • The app’s settings, including per-chat choices, GIF favourites and emoji use, are also stored on your XMPP server, in a private node that only your account can read. That is how your other devices share them. Read state and group bookmarks are synced the same way.
  • Notifications show the sender and the message on this device. The app tells iOS which chats you use, with their names and pictures, for the share sheet’s suggestions. That stays on the device.
  • The diagnostic log (Settings > Export Logs) holds connection, sending and call events, with the app version and the device model. It holds no addresses and no message text. It leaves the device only if you export it and send it. If you send it to us, we keep it only while we look at the problem, then delete it.
  • Signing out deletes the messages, keys, log and share suggestions stored on the device for that account. Messages on your server stay there.

Encryption

Yapnest uses OMEMO. With OMEMO, only you and the devices of the people in the chat can read a message. Your server cannot.

  • It is on by default in one-to-one chats. You can turn it off for a chat.
  • In group chats it is on only in private rooms, where only members can join and each member’s address is visible. Other rooms are not encrypted.
  • OMEMO encrypts the text and the files of a message, and nothing else. Your server still sees who talks to whom and when. It also sees reactions, deletions, read markers, typing, who a message mentions, and which message a reply or an edit refers to.
  • New devices of a contact are trusted automatically until you verify one of theirs. After that, you decide on each new device.

What the people you chat with see

People you added or approved see:

  • when you are online, your availability and your status message;
  • your name and photo;
  • when you received and read their messages, and when you are typing.

You can turn off read receipts and typing with “Send Read Receipts” and “Send Typing Indicator” in Settings. With “Activity Status”, which is off unless you turn it on, they also see how long ago you were online. Your name and photo are only for them, unless you turn on “Show My Name and Photo to Everyone”.

Anyone you talk to can tell that you use Yapnest, as the app names itself. They cannot tell the version or the device.

Files

Files go to the upload service of your XMPP server. They are encrypted first when OMEMO is on for the chat. If it is off, the upload service can read them. Your server decides how long files are kept.

Pictures from people you added or approved, and from members of your private groups, download as soon as they arrive, from wherever the sender uploaded them. That host sees your IP address and when you got the picture. Other files, and pictures from anyone else, wait until you tap. The setting “Download Pictures from Group Members” makes group members’ pictures wait too.

Calls

Calls go directly between the devices when they can. Then each side sees the other’s IP address. Otherwise they go through a relay (TURN) that your XMPP server offers. The relay sees both addresses and carries the media, which is encrypted (DTLS-SRTP).

  • “Always Relay Calls” in Settings hides your address from the person you call. It needs your server to offer a relay. It is off by default.
  • Your server sees who calls whom and when.
  • Calls are not listed in the phone’s Recents.
  • We are not involved in calls.

Push notifications

To tell you about a new message when the app is closed, the app uses Apple’s push service (APNs) through a relay we run, at push.yapnest.fr.

  • The app sends the relay your device’s push token and whether it is a test or a production build. The request also carries your account address.
  • The relay answers with an opaque code. The app gives that code, not the token, to your XMPP server. Your server never sees the token.
  • When your server has a new message for you, it tells the relay. The relay asks Apple to wake the app. The server’s push names your account, so the relay sees that your account got a push, and when. Some servers add the sender and the text. The relay throws them away and never sends them to Apple. The alert has no content: not the sender, not the text. The app then fetches and decrypts the message itself and shows the notification.
  • The relay stores nothing on disk. The code carries the token and your address, sealed with a key that only the relay has. The relay remembers a device for ten seconds, to avoid sending a push twice.
  • The relay’s log records only that a device registered or a push was sent, and errors. It never records your account address or your device.
  • The relay runs at Scaleway, in Paris, France.
  • The relay reaches your server through an XMPP server of ours. That server may log the domain names of the servers that connect to it, never accounts or messages.
  • Apple receives the push token and the empty alert, under Apple’s own privacy policy.

Link previews

A link preview shows a title and image for a link. The app loads the linked page from your device, so the site sees your IP address and that the link was opened. Nothing goes through us.

  • For links you send, previews are on unless you turn them off for the chat.
  • For links you receive, the app asks you per chat first.
  • Only https links are previewed.
  • The app sends no cookies and does not name itself. It fetches a page the way WhatsApp does.
  • In Settings you can turn link previews off for everything.

GIFs

The GIF picker uses KLIPY. When you open it or search, the app sends KLIPY your search words, your region as set on your device (a country code), a content filter and the page it wants, then loads the pictures from KLIPY. Every request carries Yapnest’s API key, so KLIPY knows the requests come from Yapnest. KLIPY sees your IP address. The app uses no cookies and no cache for this, and sends no user ID. Your favourite GIFs are kept as KLIPY ids in your settings, and their previews load from KLIPY when you open them. A GIF you pick is downloaded and sent as an ordinary file in your chat.

KLIPY’s own privacy policy covers what it does with this. In Settings you can turn the GIF picker off.

Location, camera, microphone, photos

  • Location: only when you choose to share it in a chat. The app reads your position then and shows it on an Apple map before you confirm, so Apple serves the map for your position when the picker opens. If you confirm, it sends the position as a message to that chat. It is not collected otherwise.
  • Maps: a location in a chat, yours or one someone sent you, is drawn with Apple Maps. To draw it, the app asks Apple for the map around those coordinates, so Apple sees them and your IP address, under Apple’s own privacy policy. The person who sent the location learns nothing from this.
  • Camera and microphone: when you take a photo or video to send, record a voice message, or are in a call. The microphone records the sound of a video and of a voice message. Nothing is recorded otherwise.
  • Photos: the app saves a photo or video to your library only when you ask it to.

You can turn each permission off in your device’s settings.

What we do not do

  • No analytics and no advertising code in the app. It uses open-source libraries for XMPP, OMEMO and its local database.
  • The app contains no crash reporter. Apple may pass us crash reports if you chose to share them with developers in your device settings. It does so for TestFlight builds too, with the feedback testers send. They name no account.
  • No tracking, and no sale of data.
  • We do not read your messages.

Why we use this data, and the legal basis

Under the GDPR, we handle the little data we get (push token and account address) because it is needed to give you the notifications you ask for. That is the performance of a contract with you (Article 6(1)(b)). Keeping the relay safe and working is our legitimate interest (Article 6(1)(f)). For features that need a permission or an outside service, such as location, camera, GIFs or link previews, you start the feature yourself, and that is your consent (Article 6(1)(a)).

How long we keep it

  • The relay stores no records, and its logs name no one. Its logs stay on its server until they are rotated out by size, a few tens of megabytes at most. Its usage counts, which hold nothing about any account, are kept for 31 days.
  • Data on your device stays until you sign out or delete the app.
  • It is also in the device’s backups (iCloud Backup, Finder, Time Machine), unless “Include Messages in Backups” is off. A backup made before keeps its copy.
  • If you delete the app without signing out, your password and this device’s encryption key stay in the Keychain on this device until you sign in and sign out.
  • Data on your XMPP server, and at KLIPY, Apple and the sites you link to, is kept as they decide.

Your rights

You can ask us for access to your data, to correct it, to delete it, to limit or object to its use, and to receive it in a portable form. You can withdraw consent at any time. To use a right, write to support@yapnest.fr. We answer within one month.

For data on your XMPP server, ask its operator.

You can also complain to the French data protection authority, the CNIL: cnil.fr/en/plaintes.

International transfers

Your XMPP server, KLIPY and Apple may be outside the European Economic Area. Their policies say how they protect data.
